# Techniques: authoring

**A node is a measurement, not a topic.**
When: inventorying nodes for a slice.
Do: mint a node only when its criterion can be demonstrated on its own and
its state would change routing (teach, skip, or review). Otherwise it is
content inside another node's material.
Evidence: knowledge components (KLI framework); locally, the no
zero-information rule applied to structure.

**Separate knowing from doing.**
When: one topic yields both an "explain X" and a "do X" candidate.
Do: two criteria, two nodes. Link them only by definitional necessity, which
often fails: procedure frequently precedes understanding.
Evidence: KLI's distinct learning processes; expert blind spot (Nathan &
Koedinger) warns against requiring understanding first.

**Ground the graph in sources.**
When: a slice's shape is drafted.
Do: verify the decomposition against syllabi, textbooks, and canonical
references by web search; harvest documented misconceptions for distractors;
record citations. Search verifies structure and instruments, never prose to
paste.
Evidence: concept inventories (Hestenes' FCI); parametric memory is the
biased instrument here.

**Slice before course.**
When: scoping a new mission's chart.
Do: first slice is the mission target's dependency cone, on the order of 10
to 25 nodes; expand on demand.
Evidence: the tutoring-precedent survey; tutorial traditions scope to the
target performance, not the discipline.

**Materials are criterion-delivery vehicles.**
When: drafting or refining any material.
Do: include the minimum exposition its worked example needs, the worked
example, faded exercises, and citations out for depth. Cut history,
motivation past one line, and decoration.
Evidence: seductive details reliably hurt novice learning (Mayer); teach to
the criterion.

**Evidence before instruction.**
When: creating or revising a node.
Do: write the criterion and at least one item before writing any material.
Evidence: backward design (Wiggins & McTighe); locally the UbD ordering from
the precedent survey.

**Definitional necessity for edges.**
When: proposing any dependency edge.
Do: ask "can a learner demonstrate B's criterion without A?" Only "no" mints
the edge. Teaching custom is not necessity.
Evidence: expert blind spot (Nathan & Koedinger); expert-derived orderings
systematically over-require.

**Flag threshold concepts.**
When: scoping a course.
Do: mark the transformative, troublesome nodes and spend extra items and
lesson budget there.
Evidence: Meyer & Land; qualitative but consistent across disciplines.

**One criterion per node.**
When: writing any criterion.
Do: if the criterion needs "and", split the node. The node is the unit of
assessment, so its grain is set by what one item can test.
Evidence: knowledge components (KLI framework, Koedinger et al.).
