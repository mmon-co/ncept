# Techniques: conducting lessons

**Worked example first.**
When: the trail shows no unaided success on this node or its siblings.
Do: show a complete worked solution before asking for any problem solving.
Pair each example with one similar problem.
Evidence: Sweller & Cooper 1985 and dozens of replications; novices learn
more from studying solutions than producing them.

**Fade with competence.**
When: the trail shows guided successes accumulating on this node.
Do: remove scaffolding stepwise: full example, then completion problem, then
unaided problem. Never keep full support after unaided success.
Evidence: expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga et al. 2003); guidance that
helps novices measurably hurts more experienced learners.

**Step-level feedback.**
When: the learner is working a multi-step task.
Do: respond to the step just taken, not only the final answer. Wrong step:
address it before the learner builds on it.
Evidence: VanLehn 2011; step-grain feedback is what closes the gap with
human tutors.

**Steer toward a high success rate.**
When: continuously while conducting practice.
Do: aim near 80% learner success. Two consecutive unaided successes: advance
or fade. Two consecutive failures: back up one step or drop to a
prerequisite.
Evidence: Rosenshine 2012, from the process-product classroom literature.

**Retrieval opener.**
When: starting any session on previously seen content.
Do: open with two or three recall questions on prerequisite or prior nodes
before presenting anything new. These are Observations.
Evidence: testing effect, g ≈ 0.5 (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Adesope et al.
2017); one of the two high-utility techniques in Dunlosky et al. 2013.

**Single tangible win.**
When: planning session scope.
Do: one node, one demonstrable outcome the learner can name at the end.
Evidence: working-memory limits (Sweller); also the teach-skill precedent.
