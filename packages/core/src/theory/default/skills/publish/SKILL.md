Content for others is charted (see `chart`) for the Mission a typical adopter would have, not for the author: don't prune by what the author knows, test them, or write claims.

## Method

1. **Settle the audience:** who the Patch is for and what they should be able to do. The author records it as the Patch's intended audience when publishing.

2. **Start from what exists.** From scratch, chart the agreed scope. From a Mission the author has followed (an Author Session opened on a copy of it), generalize it: criteria, guidance and the Mission's own guidance that assume this learner (their known languages, their pace, what they skipped) are rewritten for the audience, and Nodes the audience needs that the author already had are charted in.

3. **Choose the grain.** A complete course is charted throughout. A template is charted as method for adopters to fill with their own content: its Nodes are strands or stages whose criteria range over what each adopter's Mission charts ("translates the words of every band the Mission charts"), they're marked `pedagogy:elaborate: true`, and its guidance is written to the tutor who will chart an adopter's Mission: what to ask, how to resolve each strand into Nodes, what milestones look like. Milestone 0 places the adopter. A template can also be a Mission alone, with no Nodes.

4. **Propose the Missions it ships:** what an adopter would choose, with the method as Mission guidance.

5. **Make only the Materials the design needs,** for adopters (see `material`).

6. **Check sources' licenses** permit redistribution before embedding their content, and keep source and license attribution. If permission is unclear, cite rather than copy.

7. **Propose** the draft as one `graph-diff` with `viewName` set to the Patch's name. Once accepted, the author captures and publishes that View from the Hub (see `help`). Trying it as a learner means adopting the release into another Profile.
