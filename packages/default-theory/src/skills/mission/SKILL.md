name: mission
description: The learner is setting a new goal, or has said their goal has moved or is no longer wanted.
---

A Mission is a concretely demonstrable long-term goal the learner wants directly, paired with a View of how to get there. Finding or creating graph content is dependency resolution from the Mission, so frame the Mission right first.

## Method

1. **Read existing Missions.** A moved goal revises its Mission; an unwanted one is retired; only an unrelated goal starts a new Mission.

2. **Research the goal on the Hub and the web.** Survey high-level approaches, reading only what you don't already know. If a Hub template fits this learner's approach as well as charting from scratch, explain what it covers and how it differs, and propose charting from it.

3. **Interview the learner maieutically.** You're responsible for raising possible approaches and pointing out their blind spots. They're responsible for deciding what exactly they want. If the learner seems unaware of something relevant (eg existing materials, pedagogical approaches, or lines of thought), tell them about it rather than affirming what they feel to be novel. Don't be afraid to be a bit disagreeable. Your role is to help the learner figure out what they _really_ want to achieve, and the most effective way of doing so. Don't always take the learner's words at face value.

   Interview in waves, modeling the mission scope as a frontier of clarity expanding into fog. In each wave, summarize your current shared understanding, then ask the questions with no dependencies, including on each other. If a question depends on the answer to another question you're asking, save the dependent one for the next round. Continue until the understanding is concrete enough to resolve the Mission into Nodes:
   - what the learner wants to accomplish or be able to do, by when (if time-bound), and why
   - what's out of scope, and how their situation and motives differ from typical curricula
   - existing context—what they already know that gives a head start or enables transfers and analogies
   - pedagogical preferences informed by what has worked or failed for them, and how much say they want over method changes
   - whether they want capabilities checked on their real work rather than exercises—if so, settle what work and what standard

4. **Backbrief, propose, and move to charting.** Give a full backbrief and propose the Mission: put the learner's preferences and context in its teaching guidance, and any agreed real-world work and standard in its assessment guidance (overriding generic criteria) so later Sessions don't ask again. Unless the approach comes from an adopted Patch or an established course, record it in teaching guidance as a conjecture under test—where it comes from and what would count against it. Once accepted, start charting.
