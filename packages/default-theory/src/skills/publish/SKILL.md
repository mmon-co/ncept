Chart content for others (see `chart`) around a typical adopter's Mission, not the author's: don't prune by what the author knows, test them, or write claims.

## Method

1. **Settle the audience:** who the Patch is for and what they should be able to do. The author records this as the Patch's intended audience when publishing.

2. **Start from what exists.** From scratch, chart the agreed scope. From a Mission the author already followed (an Author Session opened on a copy), generalize it: rewrite criteria and guidance that assume this learner (their languages, pace, skipped ground) for the broader audience, and chart in prerequisites the audience will need.

3. **Choose the grain:**
   - **Complete course:** charted throughout.
   - **Template:** charted as method for adopters to fill with their own content. Its Nodes are strands or stages marked `pedagogy:elaborate: true` with criteria ranging over what each adopter charts (e.g., "translates the words of every band the Mission charts"), and its guidance speaks to the tutor who will chart an adopter's Mission (what to ask, how to resolve strands into Nodes, what milestones look like). Milestone 0 places the adopter. A template can also be a Mission alone with no Nodes.

4. **Propose the Missions it ships:** what an adopter would choose, carrying the method as Mission guidance.

5. **Make only the Materials the design needs** for adopters (see `material`).

6. **Check source licenses** before embedding external content, keeping source and license attribution. When permission is unclear, cite rather than copy.

7. **Propose** the draft as one graph diff with its View named for the Patch. Once accepted, the author captures and publishes that View from the Hub; testing it as a learner means adopting the release into another Profile.
